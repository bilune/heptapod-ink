## 1. COGS (Kim & Linzen 2020, EMNLP; repo `repos/COGS`)

### 1.1 Generation process [S]
- Task: English sentence → logical form (LF), cast as seq2seq semantic parsing (§2, §3).
- Sentences generated by a **PCFG** (§4, App. A). Uniform probability (~5%) per verb frame
  (transitive with subject+object, transitive subject-only, passive subject-only, passive with
  by-phrase, ...), except CP-embedding constructions raised to ~8% to match corpora (App. A).
  **Zipfian** distribution over lexical items within each noun/verb subcategory (App. A), so that the
  most frequent items appear in every pattern they allow.
- Sentence types (names from Roland et al. 2007, App. A): simple intransitive, to-infinitive VP,
  sentential complement, simple transitive, ditransitive, passive. Covers 70-80% of naturally
  occurring English sentence types by that analysis (§4).
- Semantics follow deterministically from PCFG rules annotated with semantic class (needed to
  disambiguate unaccusative / unergative / object-omitted transitive, §4). Verb subclasses are
  distinguishable only distributionally (animacy of subjects; App. A).
- LFs: first produced with Reddy et al. (2017) UDepLambda, then postprocessed (App. C, Table 5).
- Sampled **30,000 distinct sentences**, excluding duplicate nominals ("The cat saw a cat"), split
  80/10/10 → train 24,000, dev 3,000, test 3,000 (§4).
- Added to train: primitive LFs for 80 verbs and 60 nouns, plus 15 "primitive exposure examples"
  (one per generalization case that needs one), total 24,155 (§4).
  [M] The actual `train.tsv` has 24,000 `in_distribution` + **143 `primitive`** + **12
  `exposure_example_*`** rows = 24,155; the paper's 140 + 15 does not match the file exactly.
  `train_100.tsv` (App. E.2): 24,000 + 14,300 primitive + 1,200 exposure = 39,500.
- Generalization set: separate PCFGs per case, 1,000 sampled per case × 21 cases = **21,000** (§4).
- Changelog (README): on 2022-09-13, 50 examples of `obj_pp_to_subj_pp` were fixed (animate subjects
  of unaccusatives had been marked agent instead of theme). Results in the 2020 paper predate this.

### 1.2 Vocabulary [S] / [M]

  2019), 113 unique verbs in 6-7 types: NP-arg verbs allowing object omission (*eat*), NP-arg verbs
  not allowing it (*find*), subject-control verbs with infinitival args (*try*), CP-arg verbs

  prepositions (in, on, beside). Relation inventory and counts: agent 60,331; theme 43,263;
  recipient 16,834; ccomp 15,399; xcomp 3,191; nmod.in 7,293; nmod.on 7,112; nmod.beside 6,856.
  Whitespace-token types: source 745, target 723 (ReCOGS ships `model/src_vocab.txt` 762 lines and
  `model/tgt_vocab.txt` 729 lines including specials and index numerals).
- [M] Lengths (words / LF tokens): train 3-22 (mean 7.5) / 10-153 (mean 43.8); test 3-19 / 10-130;
  gen 3-61 (mean 9.8) / 10-480 (mean 67.6). Paper: max gold output 480 tokens (§5.2.1).

### 1.4 LF format and quirks [S] + [M]
Format (tokenized with spaces in the data):
`* cake ( x _ 4 ) ; hedgehog ( x _ 1 ) AND eat . agent ( x _ 2 , x _ 1 ) AND eat . theme ( x _ 2 , x _ 4 )`

- Neo-Davidsonian: verbs introduce an event constant; thematic roles link args to it (App. H).
  Primitive LFs use lambdas: `LAMBDA a . shark ( a )`, `Emma`,
  `LAMBDA a . LAMBDA b . LAMBDA e . touch . agent ( e , b ) AND touch . theme ( e , a )` (App. H).
- **Variable indexing tied to token position** [S] §4, App. C: constants are `x_i` with i the 0-based
  index of the phrase head in the sentence (*A cat smiled* → `cat(x_1)`). Proper nouns are not
  variables, they appear as constants (`eat.agent(x_1, Charlie)`). Chosen "to avoid the need to select
  arbitrary constant names" (§4). Consequence (§5.2.1, ReCOGS §4.3): deeper generalization examples
  require index values never seen in training.
- **Definites** are marked `*` and pulled to the left, separated by `;` (ι-terms are type e, not t,
  App. H). Indefinites are conjoined inline.
- **Conjunct order**: App. C says conjuncts are "sorted by the subscript of the Skolem constants".
  [M] Empirically the body is sorted by the *first* argument's index, with ties in surface order of
  the second argument. So the LF string encodes word order and syntax, not just meaning:
  - *A dog helped a rose* → `dog(x_1) AND help.agent(x_2,x_1) AND help.theme(x_2,x_4) AND rose(x_4)`
  - *A rose was helped by a dog* → `rose(x_1) AND help.theme(x_3,x_1) AND help.agent(x_3,x_6) AND dog(x_6)`
  - DO vs PP dative differ in recipient/theme order (`teleport.recipient ... teleport.theme` vs the reverse).
  Same truth conditions, different strings and indices.
- Event predicates triggered by nominals are removed (App. C). No VP-attached PPs; all PPs are
  NP-attached and nested, never sequential (App. H "PP attachment ambiguity").
- [M] Control verbs create **reentrancy**: *The boy needed to crawl* →
  `need.agent(x_2,x_1) AND need.xcomp(x_2,x_4) AND crawl.agent(x_4,x_1)`. 1,329 train graphs have an
  entity filling two roles. A tree or variable-free encoding loses this (ReCOGS §3).
- Evaluation: exact string match of the full LF (Fig. 2 caption). No partial credit.

## 2. ReCOGS (Wu, Manning, Potts 2023; repo `repos/ReCOGS`)

### 2.1 What is wrong with COGS LFs, per the authors [S]
1. **Redundant tokens** (`x`, `_`, parentheses, commas) distort decoder n-gram statistics: the bigram
   `, x` appears 44,846 times vs `, Emma` 4,279 (§4.2, Fig. 2). Removing `x _` alone moves the
   Transformer on Prim→Obj proper from 62.4 to 81.1 and Subj→Obj proper from 74.4 to 91.9 (Table 4).
2. **Length is confounded with depth**: deeper recursion means longer sequences and variable
   indices never seen in training (§4.3, Fig. 3). Adding k concatenated training examples (k up to
   3,072) lifts CP/PP recursion from ≈0 to ≈47-58% (Transformer) and ≈44-54% (LSTM) (Fig. 4).
3. **Variable names bound to linear position**: PP-modifier variables only ever take high indices in
   training, so the model infers PPs cannot attach to subjects (§4.4). Preposing, "um" fillers and
   participial-verb phrases (meaning-preserving) lift Obj PP→Subj PP from 0 to 8.1-88.7% (LSTM) and
   18.6-82.7% (Transformer) (Table 6).
4. They also argue the Obj PP→Subj PP split is arguably not "fair" in the Geiger et al. sense: the
   training data could legitimately support a subject/object asymmetry (§4.4, §6).
5. **Variable-free COGS (Qiu et al. 2022) is not meaning-preserving**: `need(agent=zebra,
   xcomp=walk(agent=zebra))` means "a zebra needs a zebra to walk" with two unlinked indefinites;
   it cannot express reentrancy, reflexives or quantifier binding (§3). So its high scores are
   "partly illusory".
6. COGS requires predicting exact variable identities although they are bound (existentially closed)
   and could be renamed (§1).

### 2.2 What ReCOGS changes [S] (§5, Table 7; generation in `second_looks.ipynb` cells 13-16)
1. Drop `x` and `_`.
2. Replace each position index with a random integer from the vocabulary pool, consistently within an
   example; 5 independent resamplings per training example. [M] The pool is the numerals present in
   `model/tgt_vocab.txt`: 0-54, 56, 59 (57 values); notebook cell 15 `existing_digit_pool`.
3. Add 15,360 concatenated long examples (3,072 per index resampling), labeled `length_ood`; three
   long training sentences glued without a conjunction ([M] e.g. "A baby believed that the pumpkin
   was held Olivia shortened the box ...").
4. Prepose the object in 5% of training examples with a PP (`preposing_1`), and sprinkle 1-3 "um"
   in 5% of examples (`in_distribution+sprinkle`).
5. Not experimentally motivated: proper nouns become predicates (`Emma ( 17 )`), indefinites are
   also preposed with definites, roles are separated from the event predicate
   (`eat(6) AND agent(6,7)` instead of `eat.agent(6,7)`). `nmod.in(31,36)` loses the head noun prefix.
- Example (Table 7): *A zebra needed to walk* →
  `zebra(47) ; need(13) AND agent(13,47) AND xcomp(13,48) AND walk(48) AND agent(48,47)`.
- Sizes (Table 8): ReCOGS train 135,547, dev 3,000, test 3,000, gen 21,000.
  [M] `recogs_v2/train.tsv` = 135,547 rows (112,550 in_distribution, 15,360 length_ood, 6,274
  in_distribution+sprinkle, 960 preposing_1, 137 primitive, ...). `recogs_v1` (135,546) lacks the
  preposing/um rows; `recogs_v2` is the paper's ReCOGS. `recogs_positional_index` = ReCOGS_POS
  (original indices kept, 1 resampling, 3,072 concat). Dev/test/gen are only reformatted, never
  augmented (cell 14 `translate_regular`).
- [M] Quirk: ReCOGS still orders NP conjuncts by original sentence position before index
  randomization (cell 14 sorts `def_terms` by index; cell 13 note: "NP and PP phrases mirror the word
  orders"), so conjunct order in ReCOGS still leaks surface order.

### 2.3 How evaluation works (file:line)
- **Semantic Exact Match (SEM)** [S] §5: exhaustively search for a consistent variable mapping;
  conjunctions treated as an order-free set; exact match = set equality after renaming (≈ Smatch
  without partial credit; footnote 6). Footnote 7: would need changes if LFs had tautologies,
  contradictions or equalities.
- Code: `utils/compgen.py`
  - `:9-32` `recogs_exact_match(gold, pred)`: normalize, build gold conjunct set, try every
    candidate map, rename pred variables (with a `000000` flag prefix so renames do not chain,
    `:15-21`), compare sets (`:30`).
  - `:63-86` `_candidate_variable_maps`: each variable gets a signature = sorted tuple of predicate
    names it participates in (`:89-102`); a pred variable can only map to gold variables with the same
    signature; the Cartesian product of candidates is filtered by `keys == pred_vars` and
    `values == gold_vars` (`:85`).
  - `:110-112` `get_conj_set`: split on `AND` or `;`, return a Python `set`.
  - `:115-117` `variable_change`: word-boundary regex replace.
- Wiring: `utils/train_utils.py:233-237` `check_set_equal_neoD` wraps `recogs_exact_match` in
  try/except→False. `run_cogs.py:310-313` (test) and `:368-371` (gen) use it **only if
  `--use_iiem`** (flag defined `run_cogs.py:44`); otherwise `check_equal`
  (`train_utils.py:186-221`), which renumbers numerals by first appearance and then requires an
  identical string, so it is **order-sensitive** (renaming-invariant but not conjunct-order-invariant).
  The README training commands do not pass `--use_iiem`.
- Older invariant `translate_invariant_form` (`train_utils.py:272-360`) rebuilds a nested VP
  expression per event for COGS-format LFs; `translate_invariant_form_neoD` (`:402-495`) does the same
  for ReCOGS format.
- Per-category reporting: `run_cogs.py:339-433`. `lex_acc` pools only the lexical categories *other
  than* the three proper-noun ones, which are reported separately (`:394-411`). This is the "LEX15" of
  Fig. 5.
- [M] Quirks of SEM worth knowing if we reuse it:
  - Set semantics: a prediction that repeats a conjunct still matches.
  - The map is not forced injective: if the prediction has an extra variable with an identical
    signature, two pred variables can collapse onto one gold variable and pass (e.g. `dog(1) AND
    dog(2)` vs gold `dog(5)`).
  - The signature uses only predicate names, so `*` (definiteness) is not part of the signature but
    is part of the final set comparison, which is correct.
  - Worst-case exponential in identical-signature variables; fine for COGS-sized graphs.
- `utils/cogs_utils.py:145-173` builds a "gen-dev" split by taking the longest 10% of gen (comment:
  "this is a strange partition accordingly to previous works"). [I] Using it for model selection
  leaks the generalization set. Do not copy this.

## 3. COGS/ReCOGS meanings as speaker inputs

### 3.1 Canonical structured representation [I]
Use a typed, labeled, directed graph (essentially ReCOGS semantics without any string order):

- **Entity nodes**: `{lemma, kind ∈ {common, proper}, definite ∈ {0,1}}`. Proper nouns become
  nodes, as in ReCOGS (two "Emma" mentions within a sentence are one node in COGS; keep COGS identity).
- **Event nodes**: `{verb lemma}`. No tense in COGS (all past), no polarity.
- **Edges**: event→entity `agent | theme | recipient`; event→event `ccomp | xcomp`;
  entity→entity `nmod.in | nmod.on | nmod.beside`. Reentrancy allowed (control: one entity is agent
  of both `need` and `crawl`).
- **Node identity**: none from the sentence. Assign per-example random IDs (ReCOGS step 2) or none
  at all; the speaker encoder must be permutation-invariant over nodes (set/graph encoder: GNN or set
  transformer over (head label, relation, dependent label) triples, with node-ID embeddings resampled
  every example). Shuffle triple order every time the meaning is shown.
- **Listener target**: the same graph. Score with (a) exact graph isomorphism (strict), implemented
  as SEM (`compgen.py:9`) or as a canonical hash; (b) triple F1 for diagnostics; (c) role-aware error
  types (wrong role, swapped agent/theme, wrong attachment, missing node).
- **Primitive training items** become 1-node graphs (`shark`) or a lone event node (`crawl`), which
  the speaker writes and the listener reads like anything else.

Worked example, *The boy needed to crawl*:
```
nodes: e1:EV(need)  e2:EV(crawl)  n1:ENT(boy, common, def)
edges: e1-agent->n1  e1-xcomp->e2  e2-agent->n1
```

[M] Graph sizes (entities + events): train 2-10 nodes (mode 3; 9,517 graphs), max 9 edges;
`pp_recursion` 6-18 nodes; `cp_recursion` 8-30 nodes, up to 29 edges.

### 3.2 What happens to the 21 categories when the input is the meaning [M]
I parsed every LF into this graph and compared gen to train (non-primitive rows).

- **No exact meaning leakage gen→train**: 0/21,000 gen graphs are isomorphic to a train graph.
- **Test/dev do overlap train in meaning**: 131/3,000 test and 129/3,000 dev graphs equal some train
  graph (paraphrases: active/passive, DO/PP dative), with 0 identical sentences. Inside train, 24,012
  sentences map to 23,514 distinct meanings. A meaning-input game must dedupe by canonical graph.
- **Role categories survive cleanly.** For all 11 lexical role cases, the target noun's role in train
  vs gen is disjoint, e.g. hedgehog: train {agent ×1}, gen {theme ×1000}; cockroach: train {theme ×1},
  gen {agent}; cobra: agent→theme; hippo: theme→agent; shark and Paula: no sentence in train at all.
  Syntactic subject/object in gen always map to agent/theme here, so these become clean
  "novel (filler, role)" tests.
- **Verb-frame categories survive**: `obj_omitted_transitive_to_transitive`, `unacc_to_transitive`,
  `passive_to_active`, `prim_to_inf_arg` have a novel (verb, role-set) frame in 1000/1000 gen items
  (e.g. bake seen only as {agent}, gen {agent, theme}; squeeze only {theme}).
- **Alternation categories mostly dissolve.**
  - `do_dative_to_pp_dative`, `pp_dative_to_do_dative`: **0/1000** novel frames. The DO and PP
    datives of *teleport/ship* are the same graph; the meaning-level task reduces to "new fillers
    for a verb seen once". Drop, or relabel as "rare-verb fillers".
  - `active_to_passive`: only **209/1000** have a novel frame (agentless passive, bless {theme});
    the 791 passives with a by-phrase have the same frame (bless {agent, theme}) as the single active
    exposure. Relabel as "argument omission" (209) and "rare-verb fillers" (791).
- **`obj_pp_to_subj_pp` changes meaning.** In train, PP-modified entities fill only `theme`
  (5,047 graphs; never agent, never recipient). In gen, 811/1000 put the PP on an agent or recipient
  (novel at the role level), while 189 put it only on a theme (passive or unaccusative subject:
  *The cake on the table burned*), which is already seen at the role level and novel only in the
  surrounding frame. Re-split by semantic role: "PP on agent/recipient" is the real structural test.
- **Recursion categories survive** (depth 3-12 vs 0-2) but bring a size jump: max 10 nodes in train
  vs up to 30 in gen. For a fixed-slot ring this is a capacity test as much as a structure test
  (the ReCOGS §4.3 confound, moved from sequence length to ring capacity).

### 3.3 Keeping the split clean [I]
1. Split on canonical graphs, not sentences. Hash every graph (variable-renaming and edge-order
   invariant), drop test/dev graphs that also occur in train, and report the 260 paraphrase overlaps
   separately if kept at all.
2. Rebuild the generalization categories semantically (role-filler, frame, attachment-role, depth),
   using the COGS lexical choices (hedgehog, cockroach, shark, Paula, cobra, hippo, bake, shatter,
   crawl, ...) as held-out items. Verify each held-out (lemma, role) or (verb, frame) pair has zero
   train occurrences, the same way I did, and publish the audit.
3. Keep the one-exposure design (§4 "primitive exposure examples") as a separate few-shot condition,
   and add the 100-exposure variant (Table 6) as a second condition, since COGS shows that this
   moves lexical cases a lot and structural cases not at all.
4. Separate depth from size: as in ReCOGS concat, include in training some large graphs that are
   disconnected unions of shallow graphs (same node counts as depth 3-5) so that "can the ring hold 20
   nodes" is trained, and only "nesting depth" is novel.
5. Model selection only on IID dev. Never on gen or on a gen-derived "gen-dev"
   (`cogs_utils.py:145-173`).
6. Report per category, plus a lexical/structural split (COGS README).

### 3.4 Leakage risks to flag [I]
- **Sentence-derived variable numbers** (COGS `x_i`) encode word positions and sentence length. If
  fed to the speaker, they leak syntax (passive vs active) and depth. Strip them.
- **Conjunct order** in both COGS and ReCOGS mirrors word order. Never feed the LF string; feed a
  shuffled set or graph.
- **Pretrained lemma embeddings** (GloVe, sentence-transformers, CLIP text) give held-out nouns
  (shark, Paula) prior similarity to seen nouns and can encode typical roles (selectional
  preference). That converts zero-shot primitive cases into near-interpolation. Use learned-from-
  scratch embeddings for the systematicity claim; report pretrained variants separately.
- **Per-lemma embedding table for a 1-exposure lemma**: the speaker's embedding for hedgehog is
  updated by one example. That is the intended difficulty, but also means results depend on when
  the example appears (ReCOGS §2 notes higher variance for these splits). Use many seeds.
- **Listener given the target size**: do not give the listener the number of nodes or a gold
  decoder prefix (CODEX_REVIEW §2).
- **Speaker-side generalization**: unlike COGS, the gen meaning is the speaker's input. Failure can be
  in production, in comprehension, or both. Evaluate the listener on (a) the speaker's logograms for
  gen meanings, and (b) a held-out "oracle" compositional encoding, to localize failures.
- **Selectional infelicity** exists in COGS (App. H: "the theme of eat should be edible" is not
  enforced), harmless for from-scratch models.

## 4. Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008 (PNAS 105(31):10681-10686)

### 4.1 Design [S] (Methods; "Design of Experiment 1/2")
- **Participants**: 80 university students, no linguistics background; 46 F / 34 M; mean age 22.5
  (18-40); Edinburgh ethics procedures. 2 experiments × 4 chains × 10 generations = 80.
- **Meaning space**: 27 pictures = 3 shapes (square, circle, triangle) × 3 colors (black, blue, red)
  × 3 motions (horizontal, bouncing, spiraling; arrow shows motion).
- **Signals**: lowercase strings. Generation-0 language: random labels of 2-4 syllables concatenated
  from 9 CV syllables, assigned randomly. Later outputs unconstrained (any alphanumeric string).
- **Bottleneck**: each generation's language split at random into SEEN (14 pairs) and UNSEEN (13).
  Training on SEEN only.
- **Training regime**: one session, 3 rounds, optional 2-min break between rounds. A round = 2
  randomized passes over SEEN (string alone 1 s, then string + picture 5 s), then a test. Tests in
  rounds 1-2 cover half of SEEN and half of UNSEEN; the round-3 test covers all 27 and is the only
  source of the next generation's language.
- **Transmission chain**: generation i+1 is trained on generation i's final test output, re-split
  into new SEEN/UNSEEN. Participants are not told; post-test questionnaire showed many did not even
  notice they were tested on unseen items, and none guessed the chain design.
- **Experiment 2 (expressivity filter)**: before training, if a string labeled more than one picture,
  all but one of those pairs (chosen at random) were moved from SEEN to UNSEEN, so training data were
  always 1-to-1.

### 4.2 Measures [S]
- **Transmission error**: mean normalized Levenshtein distance between each meaning's string at
  generation i and at generation i−1, over all meanings (0 = identical, 1 = maximally different).
  [I] Reconstructed formula (equation image missing in extraction):
  `E(i) = (1/|M|) Σ_m LD(s_i^m, s_{i-1}^m)`.
- **Structure**: Pearson correlation between all pairwise normalized Levenshtein distances of strings
  and all pairwise Hamming distances of meanings (0-3 features differing). Reported as a **z-score**
  against a **Monte Carlo** null of 1,000 random permutations of strings over meanings; the 95% line in
  the plots marks significance. Undefined when the language has one string for all (or all but one)
  meanings, because every permutation is equally structured.
- Also: number of distinct strings per generation (Tables 1 and 2).

### 4.3 Results [S]
- **Exp. 1** (4 chains): transmission error fell from first to last generation, mean decrease 0.748
  (SD 0.147), t(3) = 8.656, p < 0.002; some late generations reproduced the whole language exactly,
  including the 13 unseen items. Structure rose, mean increase 5.578 (SD 2.968), t(3) = 3.7575,
  p < 0.02, significantly above chance "rapidly" (Fig. 2b). Distinct words (Table 1) collapsed from 27
  to 2, 4, 5, 4 at generation 10. Outcome: **systematic underspecification** (e.g. chain language by
  gen 8: *tuge* = any horizontal motion, *poi* = any spiraling, bouncing split by shape; color
  distinctions eventually collapsed in all 4 chains).
- **Exp. 2** (filter): error fell, mean decrease 0.427 (SD 0.106), t(3) = 8.0557, p < 0.002;
  structure rose, mean increase 6.805 (SD 5.390), t(3) = 2.525, p < 0.05. Distinct words stayed high
  (gen 10: 16, 12, 12, 23; Table 2). Outcome: **compositional morphology** (Fig. 5, gen 9: substrings
  for color, shape, motion; one irregular form *renana*), present from at least gen 6, with morphemes
  lost or reanalyzed later.
- Interpretation by the authors (Discussion): learnability pressure alone gives structure but also
  ambiguity; learnability + expressivity gives compositionality. Adaptation is cumulative in
  learnability and structure, not necessarily in expressivity. Structure arises without intentional
  design.

### 4.4 Replicating with networks [I]
- Mapping: generation = a freshly initialized speaker (and listener) that learns the previous
  generation's logograms by **imitation** from a subset, then (optionally) a short communication phase.
  This is Ren et al. 2020 "neural iterated learning" (survey ref [117],
  `github.com/Joshua-Ren/Neural_Iterated_Learning`); I have not read it, check it before building.
- Protocol for our toy space first (6×6×3×2 = 1,296 meanings, or a 27-meaning 3×3×3 subset for
  a direct Kirby replication):
  1. Gen 0: random logogram per meaning (random slot choices from the mark library), or the output of
     a speaker trained only on the game.
  2. Each gen: sample SEEN ≈ 50% of meanings (Kirby used 14/27). New speaker learns meaning→logogram by
     supervised imitation on SEEN (cross-entropy on discrete slot choices), with a fixed, small step
     budget (the bottleneck must be in examples *and* in learning time).
  3. Condition A (Exp. 1 analog): imitation only. Condition B (Exp. 2 analog): filter SEEN to a 1-to-1
     mapping (drop duplicate logograms except one). Condition C: imitation + K steps of the
     reconstruction game with a fresh listener (communication as the expressivity pressure).
  4. The new speaker writes all meanings; that is the next generation's language.
  5. 4+ chains × 10+ generations, several seeds.
- Measures, same as Kirby but channel-appropriate: distance between logograms = **rotation-minimized
  edit distance** on the discrete slot sequence (min over cyclic shifts, so the ring has no privileged
  start), normalized; transmission error per generation; structure = Pearson r between meaning Hamming
  distance and logogram distance, z-scored against 1,000 permutations (Kirby Methods); distinct
  logograms per generation; accuracy of a fresh listener; held-out (UNSEEN) reproduction rate.
- Controls (also in CODEX_REVIEW §2): random codebook and hand-designed compositional code passed
  through the same chain.

### 4.5 Replicating with humans [I]
- Production is the hard part: people cannot reproduce ink logograms freehand reliably. Use a
  web interface where the participant composes a ring from the same discrete inventory the networks
  use (choose a mark and ring segment per slot; the ring can be rotated freely). That keeps the
  human and network channels identical and makes edit distance well defined.
- Kirby's regime transfers directly: 27 meanings (3 shapes or entities × 3 colors or verbs × 3
  motions or tenses), SEEN 14 / UNSEEN 13, 3 rounds of 2 exposures (logogram 1 s, then logogram +
  meaning picture 5 s), final test on all 27, 4 chains × 10 participants per condition (80 for both
  conditions), filter condition for expressivity, no mention of chains to participants, post-test
  questionnaire. Meanings must be shown as pictures, not English words, or English order leaks into
  the script.
- Optional third condition: alternate human and network generations, to test whether the network
  script is learnable by humans and vice versa (the "human-like / learnable" question in 5.6).

## 5. Survey: Peters et al., "Emergent Language: A Survey and Taxonomy" (see caveat in §0)

### 5.1 Scope and method [S]
- Systematic search 17 June 2024, PRISMA; 613 hits → 539 papers → **181 directly relevant** (§4,
  Table 2). Focus: discrete emergent language (EL) in AI, mostly multi-agent RL. Contributions:
  taxonomy (§5), unified notation and metric catalog (§6), research gaps (§7).

### 5.2 Taxonomy [S] (§5)
- **Communication setting** (§5.1, Table 3): agents (single / dual / population); cooperation
  (cooperative / semi-cooperative / competitive; competitive is rare, favors deception); symmetry
  (fixed speaker/listener roles vs role swapping; asymmetric speaker-listener setups "risk devolving
  into mere directives", [146]); recipients (targeted / broadcast).
- **Language games** (§5.2, Table 4): referential (choose target among distractors), reconstruction
  (rebuild the input, autoencoder-like; *our game is this type*), question-answer (multi-round),
  grid world, continuous world, other. They call for a standardized benchmark subset.
- **Language prior** (§5.3, Fig. 6, Table A2): evolution (no prior) vs acquisition (NL prior injected
  by supervision, or via KL divergence). Community and generational learning are an "iterative prior"
  that stays evolutionary if no designed prior is used.
- **Language characteristics** (§5.4) organized by linguistic levels (Fig. 4):
  phonetics (discrete vs continuous channel, Table 5); phonology (vocabulary type: binary, token, NL,
  sound, **picture**, Table 6); morphology (fixed vs variable length, Table 7; compression,
  redundancy/ambiguity); syntax (only 2 papers); semantics (grounding, compositionality,
  consistency, generalization; Table 8, Fig. 7); pragmatics (predictability, efficiency, positive
  signaling, positive listening, symmetry; Table 9, Fig. 8).

### 5.3 Metric families [S] (§6, Fig. 9 / B2)
- Morphology: distinct appearances (DA, eq. 5), average message length (eq. 6), active words (eq. 7),
  perplexity (eq. 8), SVD of meaning×word matrix, message distinctness (eq. 9-10).
- Syntax: syntax-tree metrics from unsupervised grammar induction (CCL, DIORA + Bayesian model
  merging; tree depth, unique preterminal groups) [193]; categorial grammar induction [102].
- Semantics / grounding: KL divergence to an NL language model [15], purity [14], RSA (Spearman over
  pairwise cosine similarities) [16].
- Semantics / compositionality: topographic similarity (eq. 17), positional disentanglement (posdis,
  eq. 18-22), bag-of-symbols disentanglement (bosdis, eq. 23-27), tree reconstruction error (TRE,
  eq. 28-31), conflict count (eq. 32-36).
- Semantics / consistency: mutual information (eq. 37-38), correlation-based (incl. variance of
  Laplacian heatmap), coherence / context independence (eq. 39-40), entropy-based consistency,
  effectiveness and normalized MI (eq. 41-45), Jaccard similarity across agents (eq. 46).
- Semantics / generalization: zero-shot evaluation (unseen inputs; unseen partners = cross-play),
  ease and transfer learning (ETL, Chaabouni et al. 2022 "at scale" [25]).
- Pragmatics: behavioral divergence (BAP/BMP, JSD to uniform), sparsity, speaker consistency,
  instantaneous coordination, message effect, causal influence of communication, inter-agent and
  within-agent divergence (IAD/WAD).

### 5.4 What it says on validity, generalization, populations, IL, grounding, visual channels [S]
- **Validity of compositionality metrics**: the taxonomy is motivated by "ill-adapted metrics" and a
  "discrepancy between the concepts intended for measurement and their corresponding metrics"
  (§5 opening). Specific limits stated: posdis assumes message length = number of attributes and one
  attribute per position [180]; bosdis assumes order is irrelevant; conflict count assumes |f| = |m|
  and breaks for negation or context-sensitive constructions [51]; TRE needs an oracle derivation and
  pretrained embeddings; grounding metrics need an oracle or NL precursor (§6.4.1); optimal values of
  metrics are unclear and "what constitutes a 'good' EL system" is "largely unanswered" (§6.6); metrics
  need "more rigorous investigation" and edge-case testing (§7.2.1, §8). They cite but do not
  re-examine Kharitonov & Baroni [121] (generalization and acquisition speed not tied to
  compositionality) and Chaabouni et al. 2020 [122]. **The survey does not run any validity
  analysis itself.**
- **Compositionality** "often requires deliberate guidance" and does not arise naturally [119]
  (§6.4.2).
- **Generalization**: languages that generalize "tend to emerge only when the input is sufficiently
  varied" [122]; a large dictionary often signals lack of generalization [108] (§6.4.4). Zero-shot has
  two forms: unseen input and unseen partner; ETL measures how fast fresh listeners learn a frozen
  language on new tasks.
- **Populations**: more regularization and evolution possibilities, more compute (§5.1); heterogeneous
  populations [118] and population effects [179] are open directions (§7.2.6); IAD/WAD for symmetry.
- **Iterated learning**: only as "community and generational learning", where older generations train
  younger ones [105, 116, 117] (§5.3). No dedicated metric section. Code for Ren et al. [117] is listed
  in Table A1.
- **Grounding**: defined as binding to environmental concepts; useful metrics require comparison to
  NL-like grounding, hence an oracle (§5.4.5, §6.4.1).
- **Visual / drawing channels**: "picture" vocabulary appears in only 5 of 181 papers
  [57 Fernando et al. drawing games, 58 Brandizzi review, 104 Qiu et al. graphical conventions,
  169 Lei et al. sketch QA, 174 Mihai & Hare learning to draw] (Table 6). Sound and graphics are rare,
  plausibly because discretization is hard (§5.4.2). No metric is specific to pictorial signals.
- **Open problems** (§7.2, nine dimensions): evaluation metrics; EL-NL alignment and the
  "Evolution-Acquisition Dilemma"; EL and LLMs; representation learning; agent design; setting design
  (scale, perceptual grounding, benchmarks); communication design (incl. "topology-aware variable
  communication channels", multimodal channels); learning strategies; human-agent interaction
  (human-in-the-loop, interpretability).

### 5.5 Bearing on circular vs linear, non-sequential signals [I]
- The survey has nothing on circular, rotation-invariant or 2D-layout signals. The closest hooks are
  (a) bosdis as the permutation-invariant alternative to posdis (§6.4.2), (b) "picture" vocabularies
  (5 papers), (c) "topology-aware variable communication channels" as future work (§7.2.7). This is a
  real gap we can claim, but it also means no off-the-shelf metric fits.
- posdis is undefined on an unanchored ring (no positions). Options: posdis after canonical rotation
  alignment (rotate each logogram to maximize agreement with a reference), or a "relative-position
  disentanglement" over ordered pairs of marks at a given angular offset; bosdis applies as is but
  ignores role binding (CODEX_REVIEW §2 makes the same point).
- Rotation invariance is not order-freeness (CODEX_REVIEW §1): the survey's posdis/bosdis dichotomy
  maps onto "linear order" vs "bag", and a ring sits in between (cyclic order). A clean way to state the
  claim: compare three channel topologies at matched capacity: line (posdis-friendly), ring (cyclic),
  bag (bosdis-friendly, positions shuffled).
- Signals that are 2D images also need a discrete transcription for any of these metrics. Our
  slot-choice representation (v2) gives one; v1's continuous brush does not.

### 5.6 Bearing on "is it human-like / learnable" [I]
- The survey's operational proxies: zero-shot to unseen partners, ETL (speed of fresh listeners), and
  human-agent interaction studies (§6.4.4, §7.2.9). It offers no standard for "human-like".
- Combine with Kirby: learnability = acquisition curve of fresh learners from a bottleneck sample
  (networks and people); structure = Kirby's permutation z-score; human-likeness = whether humans in
  the 4.5 protocol learn the network's script faster than a random code with the same inventory and
  slower or equal to a hand-designed compositional code.
- The survey flags fixed speaker-listener roles as producing directive-like protocols (§5.1). Our game
  is asymmetric; a role-swapping or population variant would address that critique.

## 6. Recommendation [I]

**Stage 1, systematicity (now): a graph-native "mini-COGS", not raw COGS strings.**
- Meanings: our own small generator over COGS-style Neo-Davidsonian graphs (agent/theme/recipient,
  ccomp/xcomp, nmod), with a reduced lexicon (e.g. 20-40 nouns, 10-15 verbs covering transitive,
  unaccusative, unergative, object-omitted, dative, control) drawn from the COGS lexicon, plus
  tense/polarity if we want to keep the v1/v2 slots.
- Held-out structure designed at the meaning level from day one: novel (filler, role) pairs
  (COGS 3.1 equivalents), novel (verb, frame) (3.4.3, 3.4.4, 3.5), PP on agent/recipient when train
  only has PP on theme (the semantic version of 3.2), depth 3-4 with size-matched shallow training
  graphs (ReCOGS §4.3 lesson), and role-reversal minimal pairs.
- Why: COGS shows lexical and structural generalization behave very differently (Table 7, Fig. 2b),
  so both must be in the first test; ReCOGS shows that representation artifacts (indices, order,
  length) can dominate results (Tables 4, 6, Fig. 4-5), which a graph input with SEM scoring avoids;
  my audit shows 4 of COGS's 21 categories collapse or shrink once the input is a meaning (§3.2),
  so the COGS split cannot be reused unchanged; and a small lexicon keeps one-exposure lemma
  embeddings from dominating the result. The survey's point that generalization needs varied input
  [122] argues for many distinct combinations per primitive rather than many primitives.
- Evaluation: SEM-style exact graph match (`compgen.py:9-32` logic, made injective), per-category
  accuracy, role-error breakdown, ≥5 seeds (COGS seed SD 6-8 points, Table 2). Add Kirby's structure
  z-score and a fresh-listener acquisition curve.

**Stage 2, external benchmark: full COGS meanings, canonicalized and re-split.**
- Use COGS/ReCOGS LFs converted to the §3.1 graph, deduplicated, with categories rebuilt as in §3.2
  (drop or relabel the two dative cases and split active→passive; re-define obj_pp_to_subj_pp by
  role). Report both 1-exposure and 100-exposure (`train_100.tsv`) conditions. This gives the
  "known benchmark" comparison point, with the explicit caveat that LF→ink→LF is not semantic parsing,
  so numbers are not comparable to Table 7 or ReCOGS Fig. 5.

**Stage 3, transmission and learnability.**
- Kirby-style chains (§4.4) on the Stage 1 meanings with the filter and communication conditions;
  human replication (§4.5) with a compose-from-marks interface on a 27-meaning subset.

**Stage 4, breadth ("say anything").**
- None of these four sources supplies broad meanings. COGS's lexicon (≈400 nouns, ≈130 verbs, 6
  sentence types) is the widest thing here, and it is still synthetic and past-tense only.
  Broad coverage needs a different source (AMR was the Codex review's suggestion; not covered by
  these notes). Keep the Stage 1 held-out structure as a fixed regression test while broadening.

**Positioning in one paragraph.** In the Peters et al. taxonomy, Heptapod Ink is a dual-agent,
cooperative, asymmetric reconstruction game with a "picture" vocabulary, fixed-length (slot) messages,
no language prior, and an evaluation centered on semantics (compositionality, zero-shot
generalization). Picture channels are 5/181 of the surveyed work and none studies rotation-invariant
or cyclic layouts, which is the novelty; the absence of metrics for such channels is also the main
methodological risk, so the ring-specific metrics (rotation-minimized distances, aligned posdis,
line/ring/bag comparison) need to be defined and validated on hand-built codes before being applied
to learned ones.
