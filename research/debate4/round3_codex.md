The strongest new finding is a **subword-complexity cliff**. The structure probes establish sparse sensitivity and uneven decoder dependence; a compositional lexicon remains unproven.

I inspected the heatmap, probe implementation/results, locked predictions, and fresh-reader implementation/log. Additional checks reconstructed the seeded swap samples and counted T5 tokens using CPU-only, bounded analyses. No training or GPU jobs were run.

Across 500 swaps, 4.76/36 slots change: 13.2%. Within-swap item Jaccard is 0.127 versus 0.004 across swaps; Tom→Mary reaches 0.300. These are repeatable **edit signatures**, not word spellings: items record only destination `(slot, mark)`, omitting the source mark. Different targets, positions and mark marginals make the between-swap comparison an inadequate null. Bootstrap sentences/templates; the 1,225 pairwise overlaps per swap are dependent. Target-word presence averages 89.4%, but complete reconstruction only 64.2%; presence alone does not establish successful substitution with everything else preserved. Top-five concentration of 32.5–54.8% also needs a null that selects its own top five.

The reconstructed samples expose substantial confounding. Tom starts 34/50 sentences; today ends 42/50. Eligible source pools range from 334,363 Tom sentences to 736 Paris sentences. These are Tatoeba **training-pool** examples, with no template-disjoint control or frequency matching. Eat→drink changes the most slots, 6.96, and removes one T5 token in **all 50** examples; the other nine swaps preserve token counts throughout. Token shifts could explain part of its apparent semantic footprint. Regex boundaries also admit possessives and compounds, including “Tom’s” and “two-thirds.”

Of 4,320 pokes, 83.1% leave greedy text unchanged, 10.9% change one word, and 2.2% change at least half. Conditional on any effect, those last two rates are approximately **64.5% and 13.0%**. This rejects an obligatory avalanche response, but unchanged output can reflect redundancy, ignored symbols or decoder priors. Pokes use uniformly random alternative symbols, potentially outside the writer’s distribution; they compare against the baseline decoding, not the source. Indexwise word comparison turns insertions into apparent cascades and excludes appended words from position statistics.

The heatmap’s row normalization hides sample sizes: slot 9 affects only **5/120** sentences and slot 35 **10/120**, versus **60/120** for slot 17. Slot 6 is incorrectly described as start-oriented: its mean affected position is **0.799**. End effects at 14/28/33 are stronger, with means 0.959/0.940/0.968 and 32/23/37 affected sentences. The 0.208 within-slot spread versus 0.289 continuous-uniform reference lacks a length- and activity-matched null. Learned slot queries, positional embeddings and independent per-slot codebooks already permit positional specialization; circular appearance establishes no nonsequential grammar.

These probes concern nearest-centroid codes. Search averages 13.16 accepted moves on the locked natural set—moves can revisit slots or be reverted—so its code structure needs separate measurement. Recounting predictions gives **951 newly exact sentences, zero lost**, with uncased exact 33.45→81.0%. The no-worse search guard makes zero losses partly designed. “The mites missed it” repairs “cues”; “GM finally closed the plant” repairs “GM never closed the factory.” This demonstrates recovery of specific details. Correct the summary’s error denominator: **390 cased failures**, 380 uncased.

Fresh-reader evidence is also qualified. At 200k examples, plain→searched web exact is 25.39→23.44%, but **WER worsens 34.2→44.7%**; cased exact slightly improves, 22.66→23.05%. The original reader retrieves slot-specific VQ vectors, while `FreshReader` uses one shared mark embedding plus position. It must learn arbitrary slot-dependent meanings through subsequent layers. Dataset size also changes alongside training steps. This is neither a matched architecture comparison nor evidence of an unlearnable code.

I would prioritize these five tests. They are proposals only; each has a hard ceiling of 15 H100 minutes, including preparation/evaluation, with tiny CPU smoke checks and a stall watchdog. Use fresh dev material; keep the locked exam descriptive.

1. **A lexicon may exist only within positional contexts.** Encode 240 balanced minimal pairs crossing frequent/rare words with initial/medial/final placement; match T5 length and split sentence templates. Estimate edit signatures on half, then transplant their marks into held-out recipient codes. Measure target replacement **and preservation of all other words**, against same-size random patches and position/frequency-matched permutations. Portable patches support lexical components; position-bound success supports contextual vocabulary; vanishing effects implicate templates. Repeat on matched v3/v4 vocabulary if checkpoints permit; current corpora and slot counts prevent ranking their compositionality. Use controlled Hamming/edit-distance topographic similarity as supporting evidence: [Chaabouni et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407/) show that generalization and compositionality can dissociate; [Carmeli et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.189/) motivate explicit concept mappings.

2. **Boundary slots may control termination or punctuation.** On 120 new sentences, cross terminal punctuation removal with matched-length final-word substitutions and clause-fronting. Poke slots 14/28/33, 9/35 and activity-matched controls; compare teacher-forced EOS/punctuation probabilities and edit-aligned lexical changes. Permute affected positions within length strata, preserving per-slot hit counts, to obtain a positional null. Predominant EOS/punctuation effects support framing; effects tracking relocated words support lexical anchoring; broad effects after alignment support shared control. Report affected-sentence counts alongside normalized heatmaps. Symbolic tests isolate this from ring geometry.

3. **The code may devote causal influence to linguistic surprise.** All messages occupy 36 slots, so “more marks” is the wrong observable. For 240 controlled pairs, obtain frozen external-LM word surprisal and measure code Hamming distance plus each word’s teacher-forced NLL increase under same-slot donor substitutions. Regress influence on surprisal, controlling word identity/frequency, position, T5 pieces and sentence length. Residual positive association supports predictive allocation; an effect explained by tokenization supports representational load; no association rejects this simple account. [Prediction–compression equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668) motivates the hypothesis but does not establish it for this lossy fixed-length system.

4. **Reader mismatch may masquerade as recipient-specific search.** Cache 20k plain pairs; compare fresh readers with shared versus independently initialized slot×mark tables, identical data and decoder initialization, and 6k updates each. Cap each arm at four minutes and compare matched completed updates. Evaluate both and the original reader on the same 128 plain/searched dev rings, through symbols and frozen eyes; save paired predictions and bootstrap WER differences. Better transfer with slot-specific tables implicates architecture. A remaining symbolic search penalty supports distribution shift or original-reader specialization; a visual-only penalty implicates eyes/soft-input mismatch. Two weak readers leave learnability unresolved. A subsequent matched search-code exposure experiment must distinguish distribution shift from specialization.

5. **Long-sentence failure may track subword load and search limits.** Recounting shows 95.8% exact for ≤10 T5 tokens (716 items), 57.2% for 16–20 (376), and 8.9% for ≥21 (90), including EOS. Inputs span only 4–31 tokens, below the 64-token cap. At exactly **12 words**, ≤1.5 pieces/word excluding EOS gives **101/157 exact (64.3%)**, versus **5/55 (9.1%)** above it. On 128 dev items matched by word count, corpus, frequency and LM surprisal, compare symbolic/visual errors, teacher-forced/greedy decoding, and 20/40 search rounds. More rounds rescuing dense strings implicates optimization budget; teacher-forced success with greedy failure implicates autoregressive instability; persistent symbolic loss implicates representation/reader limits, without proving an information-theoretic ceiling.

I would lead with **“A learned visual code has a subword-complexity cliff.”** Make paired plain/search heatmaps of exact accuracy by word count and T5-token count, annotating cell sizes, plus the 12-word contrast with Wilson intervals. Label the analysis exploratory and the exam tokenizer-filtered. The association is striking; rarity, names and corpus remain competing explanations. It is the most concrete new result here, and it identifies a sharper next experiment than another global compositionality score.
